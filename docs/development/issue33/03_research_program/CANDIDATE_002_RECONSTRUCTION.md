## 1. Purpose

This is the first reconstruction under
[FOUNDATIONAL_DEFINITIONS.md](FOUNDATIONAL_DEFINITIONS.md) 0.1. It does not
presume that the inherited *effective exit* formulation is one valid problem.

The current step extracts a factual societal situation without importing a
norm, architectural conclusion, or target-architecture answer.

## 2. Defect in the inherited formulation

The inherited formulation asked when service dependence becomes problematic
because departure creates *disproportionate loss of essential use value* and
whether time, cost, alternatives, security, privacy, or continuity make that
loss *reasonable*.

Those terms combine distinct judgements:

| Inherited term | Hidden decision |
|---|---|
| Disproportionate | Which comparison and distribution make a loss excessive? |
| Essential | Which functions or interests receive priority? |
| Reasonable time or cost | Who bears how much time or cost, under which threshold? |
| Effective exit | Which retained capabilities count as a successful departure? |
| Legitimate requirement | Which authority or norm makes a competing purpose valid? |

None of these terms may define the factual situation before its test and
normative authority are explicit.

## 3. Evidence that remains usable

The inherited dossier contains source-grounded observations in several domains:

- social-platform relationships and network state may not continue after a
  platform change;
- mobile-ecosystem data, applications, subscriptions, settings, and connected
  device relations may not continue across ecosystems;
- cloud data may be exportable while interfaces, integrations, and operational
  state do not continue in another environment;
- platform reputation may not follow a worker to another labour platform;
- an exported file may preserve bytes while losing structure, semantics,
  permissions, relationships, or executable function;
- migration within a shared protocol, as observed in the Fediverse study,
  provides counterevidence against technical impossibility.

These observations support the existence of discontinuity in bounded domains.
They do not establish one Internet-wide problem, a zero-loss norm, or one common
architectural cause.

## 4. Elements removed from the factual core

The following may affect departure but are not, by themselves, digital
data-architecture properties:

- prices and contractual exit fees;
- satisfaction, loyalty, and product quality;
- time needed to learn a genuinely different product;
- loss of a provider's unique algorithms or service performance;
- device or product differentiation without a data-state dependency;
- market concentration and general competition conditions;
- a legal conclusion that a restriction is proportionate or justified.

They remain context or possible consequences. They cannot be merged into the
candidate without separately passing the DEC-012 admission test.

## 5. Approved factual situation

> **A person or organisation changes or ceases a digital service. Digital state
> that functioned within the original service cannot be used with the same
> meaning, relationships, authority, or operational effect outside that
> service. The affected function therefore ends or must be reconstructed.**

**Decision status:** Approved as the candidate 2 factual core under DEC-013.

This statement does not yet decide:

- that the state belongs to the user;
- that it must be transferable;
- that the function must be reproduced by another provider;
- that every reconstruction effort is a loss;
- that the situation is unacceptable;
- that one architectural property causes every observed manifestation.

## 6. Candidate factual dimensions

The broad phrase *digital value* is not yet an admissible fact field. The
observable dimensions are separated provisionally:

| Dimension | Observable discontinuity |
|---|---|
| Data availability | Data present in the original service is unavailable after departure. |
| Semantic continuity | Exported data no longer carries sufficient structure or meaning for the same operation. |
| Identity continuity | A digital identifier or its history is not recognised outside the original service. |
| Relationship continuity | Recorded relationships, membership, reach, or shared state do not continue. |
| Authority continuity | Permissions, delegations, licences, or other powers are not recognised. |
| Reputation continuity | Recorded history used to establish standing or trust does not continue. |
| Workflow continuity | A process depending on the state cannot continue without reconstruction. |
| Temporal continuity | The operation is interrupted during or after service change. |

These dimensions may later prove to be different candidates or manifestations
of one shared mechanism. That question remains open.

## 7. First boundary decision — resolved

The inherited *effective exit* family can be narrowed in two ways:

### Option A — data-architecture core

Limit the candidate to functional discontinuity caused by service-bound digital
state. Treat prices, learning costs, product differences, devices, and general
competition as context or separate research objects.

### Option B — broad switching-cost family

Retain every technical, financial, relational, organisational, and behavioural
departure cost in one candidate.

Option B does not satisfy the currently Approved research-object boundary
without multiple additional architectural contribution chains. DEC-013 adopts
Option A. Option B remains rejected for this candidate and is not silently
reintroduced through the meaning of loss.

## 9. Approved plain-language analysis approach

The terms *subject–state model* and *user-bound digital value* are suspended as
primary explanations. Candidate 2 is tested through one concrete question:

> **Which digital things exist, who is involved, and what stops working when the
> service provider changes?**

Each case records:

1. the digital thing, such as a shared document, credential, reputation record,
   conversation, permission, or workflow;
2. every person or organisation involved;
3. what each party already does or represents in that digital thing;
4. which meaning, relationship, authority, history, or function stops working
   when the provider changes;
5. which difference is merely a feature of another product and is therefore not
   part of this candidate.

Being involved does not automatically establish ownership, exclusive control,
portability, deletion authority, or a zero-loss entitlement.

### 9.1 Why multiple parties matter

A shared document may have a maker, contributors, readers, an organisation that
granted access, and a provider that stores the working version. A credential may
be used by one person but validated by another organisation. Reputation may
concern one person while incorporating statements or transactions from others.

The departure of one party cannot silently erase or rewrite the existing roles
of the others. The analysis therefore asks whether changing the provider alone
causes those roles or their digital effects to stop working.

### 9.2 Proposed problem wording for case testing

> **When a service provider changes, existing digital contributions,
> relationships, authorities, or proofs can stop working because only the
> original provider's system recognises them.**

This wording remains Proposed until concrete cases show that it describes one
factual core without absorbing product differences or unrelated rights issues.

## 10. Concrete case 1 — shared document

**Human calibration outcome:** Accepted on 2026-08-09 as the required level of
nuance and clarity.

### Digital thing

A shared document consists not only of file content, but may also contain
contributions, contributor attribution, version history, comments, access
roles, open proposals, links to other shared objects, and an active
collaboration relation.

### Involved parties

- maker or initiator;
- contributors;
- readers and editors;
- an organisation that may grant access;
- the service provider that stores and executes the working representation.

### Provider-bound discontinuity

After a provider change, file content may remain while contribution history,
attribution, comments, permissions, proposals, links, and continuous
collaboration stop working because the receiving service cannot recognise or
execute them as the same connected whole.

### Product differences excluded

Interface design, spell checking, fonts, provider-specific AI features, menus,
and search behaviour are different product features. Their absence is not loss
within candidate 2.

### Case-specific factual statement

> **When a shared document moves to another provider, existing contributions,
> access roles, discussions, and history may stop working because only the
> original provider recognises them as one connected digital whole.**

This case supports continued testing of the common candidate wording. It does
not yet establish the proposed norm or an Internet-wide architectural deficit.

## 11. Concrete case 2 — digital diploma

### Digital thing

A digital diploma may include a visible file plus the recorded connection
between a person, an educational institution, a qualification, an issue event,
and information that allows others to check that the statement is authentic and
unchanged.

### Involved parties

- the person who received the diploma;
- the educational institution that issued it;
- a person or organisation that later checks it;
- the technical service that stores, presents, or verifies it.

### Provider- or institution-bound discontinuity

The visible file may remain while the ability to check who issued it, to whom,
when, and whether it remained unchanged disappears with the original system.
Independent proof of historical facts is therefore one relevant part of this
case, but it is not adopted as the complete candidate core.

### Product differences excluded

Wallet appearance, sharing menus, notifications, and provider-specific overview
features are product differences. The recipient also does not gain authority to
rewrite the educational institution's statement.

### Case-specific factual statement

> **A digital diploma file may remain while its function as checkable proof
> disappears because the connections between recipient, issuer, issue event,
> and unchanged statement exist only in the original system.**

## 12. Required comparison before any new case

The diploma case may not replace the shared-document case. No broader core is
adopted until both are explained by the same bounded factual statement.

| Question | Shared document | Digital diploma |
|---|---|---|
| What may remain? | Text or downloaded file | PDF or image |
| What may disappear? | Contributors, comments, versions, access roles, and active collaboration | Issuer, recipient, issue event, unchanged-state check, and independent verification |
| Which function stops? | Continuing shared work | Functioning as checkable evidence |
| What is the common dependency hypothesis? | The original system is the only place where the connected roles and history work | The original system is the only place where the connected roles and issue history can be checked |

### Approved working meaning of digital source

> **The digital source is the original digital content or record on which
> further connections and functions are built.**

For the shared document, the source includes the stored document content. For
the diploma, it includes the original digital record of the issued statement.

### Approved two-route factual observation

> **When a digital system changes, is replaced, or disappears, the digital
> source itself may be lost in part or in full. Even when the digital source is
> preserved, the connections to involved people, their roles, and the history
> may be lost in part or in full. As a result, the digital whole may lose one or
> more functions in part or in full.**

DEC-015 adopts this observation without deciding that both routes have one
mechanism or form one problem core.

| Loss route | What changes | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Source loss | Original content or record is no longer available in part or in full. | Document content or the original diploma record disappears. |
| Connection loss | Source remains, but recorded people, roles, actions, or history no longer work with it. | Document collaboration or diploma verification disappears. |

The active task is to test whether the two routes can occur independently and
therefore require separate problem cores.

## 13. Approved provisional split

DEC-016 approves the following two factual cores for separate testing:

### 2A — loss of the digital source

> **A digital source may be lost in part or in full when the system on which its
> continued existence depends changes, is replaced, or disappears.**

### 2B — loss of connections around a preserved digital source

> **A digital source may remain while connections to involved people, their
> roles, and the history are lost in part or in full, causing one or more prior
> functions to be lost.**

The split is provisional. 2A must first be compared with existing outage,
durability, and recovery candidates. 2B receives a third concrete case only
after that audit.

## 14. 2A overlap audit — outage and recovery

The comparison uses dossier 009 as pre-DEC-012 source material, not as current
problem authority.

| Object | Plain-language question | Source may remain? | Requires an incident? |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 2A — source loss | Does an intact retrievable copy of the digital source remain after the system changes, is replaced, or disappears? | No | No |
| Former 9A — shared outage | Can several digital functions stop together because they share one dependency? | Yes | Yes |
| Former 9B — failing emergency and recovery means | Do the means needed to continue or recover fail with the primary system? | Yes or no | Yes |

### Independence findings

- 2A can occur during an orderly service closure or migration without a shared
  outage or failed emergency response.
- A 9A shared outage can occur while every digital source remains intact.
- A 9B recovery failure can interrupt a function without permanently losing its
  source.
- 2A and 9B overlap when failed recovery leaves no intact retrievable source.

### Proposed boundary

For this reconstruction, 2A means:

> **After a system change, replacement, or disappearance, no intact retrievable
> copy of the digital source remains within the observed case.**

Temporary unavailability is not 2A. Simultaneous failure of multiple functions
is not 2A unless the source itself is lost. Failed emergency or recovery means
are a possible cause of 2A, not the same factual core.

### Preliminary result

2A is not fully contained in the former 9A or 9B candidates. It remains a
separate factual core for continued research, with an explicit overlap link to
9B where recovery failure results in source loss. Rootness and architectural
contribution remain unassessed.

**Human outcome:** Approved as the 2A factual boundary under DEC-017. 2A is
limited to actual source loss. Temporary unavailability and failed recovery
remain outside 2A unless no intact retrievable source remains.

## 15. Withdrawn calibration angle — online reputation

### Digital sources

The source material may include individual reviews, recorded transactions, work
history, dispute outcomes, and other events from which a platform presents a
reputation.

The platform's aggregate score, ranking formula, recommendation model, and
market reach are separate provider-specific products. They are not assumed to
be the same digital source or function.

### Involved parties

- the person or organisation whose history is presented;
- reviewers, customers, clients, or transaction partners;
- the platform that records events, checks participation, and presents a score;
- a future party that uses the history to decide whether to interact.

### Proposed 2B discontinuity

Reviews or transaction records may remain readable after a platform change,
while the new system cannot establish which verified transaction they followed,
who submitted them, whom they concern, when they occurred, or whether they were
changed. The source text remains, but its former reputation function may be lost
because the recorded connections are no longer recognised.

### Product differences excluded

- the old platform's score calculation;
- position in the old platform's ranking;
- recommendation algorithms;
- interface and presentation;
- the size or composition of the new platform's market.

A five-star score for one type of activity is not automatically equivalent to a
five-star score for another activity. The factual test concerns preserved source
records and their checkable connections, not mandatory reuse of one provider's
judgement.

### Case-specific factual statement

> **Reviews or transaction records may remain visible after a platform change
> while their connections to the reviewer, transaction, reviewed party, time,
> and prior checks stop working. The records can then lose part or all of their
> prior function as reputation evidence.**

### Human review outcome

The case is **not accepted as an independent calibration angle** under DEC-018.
Reviews and transaction records are contributions by involved actors and were
already present in the shared-source analysis. Presenting them under the label
*reputation* adds no new actor relation, loss route, mechanism, or conclusion.

The platform's aggregate reputation score or ranking is a separate derived
platform output. It cannot be included in 2B without a new analysis beginning
from its actors and source records.

The example may later illustrate 2B, but it does not independently validate or
broaden 2B and is not counted as a third calibration case.

## 16. Approved factual core 2B

DEC-019 adopts:

> **When a digital system changes, is replaced, or disappears, the digital
> source may remain while recorded connections to involved people and
> organisations, their roles, contributions, actions, and history are lost in
> part or in full. As a result, the digital whole may lose one or more functions
> in part or in full.**

2A actual source loss and 2B connection and function loss are separate factual
cores.

## 17. Approved human norms

The norms below judge loss caused by a system change. They do not determine that
a source, role, permission, or relationship must exist forever. A separately
decided change to the source or relationship is a different event and requires
its own authority and norm.

### Approved norm 2A — zero source loss caused by system lifecycle

> **Changing, replacing, or ending a digital system must cause zero loss of a
> digital source. Whether the source itself continues or ends must be decided
> separately from whether the system continues or ends.**

This means that system lifecycle alone may not make the continued existence of
the source depend on the provider's continued existence. It does not yet decide
who may deliberately end a source or under which conditions.

### Approved norm 2B — zero connection loss caused by system lifecycle

> **Changing, replacing, or ending a digital system must cause zero loss or
> alteration of the recorded connections between a digital source and involved
> people or organisations, their roles, contributions, actions, and history.**

The connections must remain recognisable and checkable. The norm does not freeze
a role or relationship forever and does not require retention of a
provider-specific interface, score, ranking, algorithm, or product feature. It
only prohibits the system transition itself from silently changing or erasing
the recorded connections.

DEC-020 approves both norms. The following authority and scope questions remain
open without weakening the zero-loss requirement for system lifecycle:

1. Does zero loss apply absolutely to every source, or only while the source is
   independently designated to continue?
2. Is preservation of 2B connections sufficient, or must every non-provider-
   specific function based on them also continue?
3. Which human or organisational actors may decide that a source, role,
   permission, or relationship ends?

These questions may not be hidden inside *reasonable*, *valid*, *necessary*, or
similar qualifiers.

## First Bounded Architecture Test — Preliminary Result (2026-08-09)

The approved 2A and 2B cores have been tested against Git, IPFS, Solid Protocol, and W3C Verifiable Credentials Data Model 2.0.

The comparison supports keeping 2A and 2B separate:

- source identity, replication, and persistence address 2A;
- portable links, roles, history, and verification relationships address 2B;
- the tested architectures solve different parts of these problems;
- none of their core models guarantees both approved zero-loss norms.

The test does not approve a new factual problem, mechanism, or target architecture. It produces two provisional mechanism hypotheses for evidence testing.

Authoritative test record: [Candidate 002 Architecture Test](CANDIDATE_002_ARCHITECTURE_TEST.md).


### Methodological Correction — Primary Subject versus Comparative Control

The tested alternative architectures are not evidence that commonly used platforms dysfunction. Their existence only shows that parts of 2A and 2B may be technically avoidable and supplies mechanisms for comparison.

The primary evidence must come from commonly used platforms and dependencies themselves: actual loss events, affected functions, human dependence, recovery limits, recurring platform patterns, and the architectural contribution to those outcomes.

This correction is governed by DEC-021 and supersedes any reading of the preliminary comparison as direct problem evidence.

### Corpus-wide result

The original generated report mainly supplies broad hypotheses about platform dependence, portability, data sovereignty, and alternative architectures. Most of those passages do not directly establish 2A or 2B under the current definitions.

Terms that must not be silently reused include:

- `bronverlies` when it means reduced traffic, visibility, attribution, or economic viability rather than disappearance of the digital source;
- `lock-in` or poor portability without evidence that a source or recorded connection was actually lost;
- continuity and outage risks that do not result in source loss;
- Solid, AT Protocol, Matrix, and similar alternatives as proof that commonly used platforms dysfunction.

### Concrete case recovered from the original corpus: De Digitale Stad

#### Factual reconstruction

De Digitale Stad (DDS) was an early Dutch online community. A complete one-to-one server copy was made in 1996. The community platform itself ended in 2001, after which its houses, squares, and cafés disappeared from public operation.

The preservation copy subsequently fell out of view. Recovering it required finding old tapes, compatible reading equipment, original servers, former system administrators, and specialist reconstruction work. The cleaned copy was eventually extracted and substantial parts of the system were reconstructed.

Sources:

- [Marleen Stikker — Het internet is stuk](https://waag.org/sites/waag/files/2025-02/MarleenStikker-HetInternetIsStuk.pdf), especially pages 79–81 and 117–119;
- [Alberts, Went & Jansma (2017) — Archaeology of the Amsterdam digital city](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/25833763/Archaeology_of_the_Amsterdam_digital_city.pdf), DOI 10.1080/24701475.2017.1309852;
- [Dutch National Library — De Digitale Stad](https://www.kb.nl/ontdekken-bewonderen/topstukken/de-digitale-stad).

#### 2A classification

**Current result: not a confirmed 2A case for the preserved 1996 snapshot.**

The source was difficult to find and unusable for years, but an intact copy ultimately remained and was recovered. Under the approved 2A definition, temporary or prolonged inaccessibility is not source loss when an intact retrievable copy still exists.

The corpus states that large parts of the early web were lost, and the 1996 snapshot does not establish preservation of all DDS material created through the 2001 closure. The available material does not yet identify which specific DDS sources, if any, left no intact copy. That remains a bounded evidence gap and must not be inferred.

#### 2B classification

**Current result: directly relevant, but only partial support pending exact boundary review.**

The peer-reviewed reconstruction shows that retaining a one-to-one data copy did not retain the whole working function:

- the original hardware and software context was required to run the data;
- source-code locations were unclear and some source code may not have been preserved;
- oral history and tacit knowledge from former inhabitants and administrators were needed to interpret the files;
- the authentication and login context could not be fully restored, leaving the reconstruction limited to guest mode;
- static web snapshots did not retain the dynamic behaviour of the community.

This is strong evidence that preserving visible data alone can fail to preserve the functioning digital whole. It touches 2B where identity, inhabitant status, participation, contributions, and history can no longer be recognised or used in the original way.

However, the paper does not yet establish precisely which recorded person-role-contribution links were deleted, as opposed to remaining present but unusable because the execution context was missing. The execution-context loss must not be silently added to 2B as a new approved component.

### Other leads retained, but not evidence conclusions

1. **Web and social-media archiving:** the source argues that static page archives miss dynamic and linked functions. This is a relevant 2B lead but requires broader evidence beyond the DDS case.
2. **Government collaboration archives:** the ODI source reports that easy message archiving and replaceability are current government requirements. This identifies an institutional concern, not an observed 2A or 2B loss case.
3. **Platform-controlled historical data:** the original corpus raises the risk that private platforms control continued access to socially relevant history. The corpus does not quantify actual source or connection loss.
4. **Alternative architectures:** Solid, ActivityPub, AT Protocol, Matrix, and personal data spaces remain solution and counterexample leads only.

### Evidence value of this pass

The original corpus did contain a valuable concrete lead that the later generated research had not classified correctly: DDS separates data copying from preservation of a usable digital whole.

It does not yet prove a general problem across commonly used platforms, nor does it prove 2A. It provides:

- one well-documented historical platform case;
- a direct warning against equating backup with preservation;
- a possible 2B mechanism involving identity, runtime context, and tacit knowledge;
- a precise gap for the next targeted research step.

### Next targeted questions

1. In the DDS record, which user, contribution, relationship, or post-1996 sources were permanently lost rather than temporarily inaccessible?
2. Does digital-preservation research find the same source-versus-function distinction across other platform types?
3. Which commonly used platforms make complete preservation of source and recorded connections possible through their standard export or handover path?
4. Does execution-context loss belong inside approved 2B, or is it a distinct factual core that must remain separate unless explicitly approved?

## Approved External Research Design — Causal Pattern First

**Decision:** DEC-024  
**Status:** ACTIVE

### Main question

Which recurring digital-data architecture patterns are shown by existing empirical research to cause, materially contribute to, or form a necessary condition for approved 2A or 2B loss?

### 2A question

Which architecture patterns are shown to result in no intact retrievable copy of a digital source remaining after a system changes, is replaced, or ends?

### 2B question

Which architecture patterns are shown to result in a digital source remaining while recorded connections to people or organisations, roles, contributions, actions, or history are lost?

### Evidence priority

1. systematic reviews and evidence syntheses;
2. multi-case or longitudinal empirical studies;
3. institutional datasets and direct preservation studies;
4. single documented cases only for unresolved gaps.

### Causal grades

- **CAUSALLY_DEMONSTRATED:** a comparison, intervention, natural experiment, or direct mechanism test substantially excludes alternatives;
- **STRONGLY_SUPPORTED:** independent studies reproduce the same complete mechanism chain;
- **ASSOCIATION_ONLY:** pattern and outcome co-occur without adequate causal exclusion;
- **HYPOTHESIS_ONLY:** technically plausible but not empirically tested.

Only the first two grades qualify for a later neutral platform cross-section.

### Required mechanism chain

architecture pattern → lifecycle event → inability to preserve source or approved connection → actual loss outcome → alternatives and mitigations tested.

### Later platform-test boundary

A platform cross-section will test whether a qualifying pattern is broadly present and effectively mitigated. Pattern presence establishes exposure, not observed loss frequency.

No architecture pattern currently passes the DEC-024 threshold for 2A.

The strongest reviewed evidence is relevant but insufficient:

1. [Strecker et al. (2023), *Disappearing repositories*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06712) identified 191 closed research-data repositories. Ninety maintained neither limited access nor a named successor repository. The authors classify these as cases where data loss *might* have occurred. They did not establish that no intact retrievable copy remained.
2. [Laakso, Matthias and Jahn (2021), *Open is not forever*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11933) verified 176 vanished open-access journals. Its operational definition permits individual issues, paper copies, commercial copies, or up to half of the content to remain. It therefore measures loss of comprehensive open availability, not approved 2A.
3. [Corry (2021), *As in Life, So in Death*](https://doi.org/10.5210/spir.v2021i0.11889) used 52 interviews with employees of closed platforms and documents shutdown-time deletion of user content. It does not verify whether an intact independently retrievable copy remained for the affected sources.

These sources jointly support a preservation-risk hypothesis: when one operating organisation holds effective custody and no independently verifiable preservation or successor arrangement exists, system closure can make sources inaccessible and may lead to loss. They do not prove the final 2A outcome required by the current definition. The hypothesis remains **ASSOCIATION_ONLY** and does not authorize platform sampling.

### Candidate 2B

One bounded pattern passes the causal threshold for a specific implementation:

> **An object-centred, single-account export can preserve a person's own content while omitting stable identifiers and cross-person links needed to reconstruct participants, contributions, responses, and shared history.**

#### Direct mechanism evidence

[Nicholas and Weinberg (2019), *Data Portability and Platform Competition*](https://www.law.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/Data%20Portability%20and%20Platform%20Competition%20-%20Is%20User%20Data%20Exported%20From%20Facebook%20Actually%20Useful%20to%20Competitors.pdf) directly inspected and reverse-engineered a Facebook Download Your Information export and tested it with approximately twelve developers, product managers, executives, and investors.

The export retained, among other things, the exporting user's comments, event records, and friend names. It omitted links required to identify other participants reliably, reconnect a comment to the specific source object, reconstruct the full conversation, reconcile separately exported copies of the same event, or reproduce attendee relations. The digital source fragment remained, while approved 2B connections did not survive the handover representation.

Because the missing identifiers and relation records directly determine the failed reconstruction, this specific implementation is graded **CAUSALLY_DEMONSTRATED**. The grade applies to the tested Facebook export model, not yet to commonly used platforms as a class.

#### Independent support and counterexample

- [Syrmoudis et al. (2021)](https://petsymposium.org/popets/2021/popets-2021-0051.php) tested export and import capabilities across 182 online services. It confirms that provider-to-provider transfer infrastructure and compatible imports were broadly incomplete, but it did not measure approved 2B relation loss and is therefore supporting context only.
- [OECD (2021)](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2021/10/data-portability-interoperability-and-competition_f09a402e/73a083a9-en.pdf) independently synthesizes why user-only exports lose value and context when links involving other users are omitted. This supports the mechanism but is not a separate direct causal test.
- [Bin Zia et al. (2024)](https://tysong.github.io/files/IMC24-Switch.pdf) observed 8,745 Mastodon account migrations. Mastodon's migration path retains identity, followers, and followees and redirects the old account, while old posts do not migrate. This is a useful control: relation-preserving migration is technically possible, and content and relation continuity are separate architectural choices.

### Current decision consequence

- **2A:** retain as an open factual candidate with a precise evidence gap. Do not run a platform prevalence test yet.
- **2B:** proceed to a predeclared neutral platform cross-section, but test only the qualifying pattern: whether the normal exit or handover path preserves source fragments together with stable actor, contribution, response, role, and history links in a form another system can reconstruct.
- Pattern prevalence will establish architectural exposure, not the frequency of actual loss events.


### Predeclared 2B Platform Cross-Section

**Freeze date:** 2026-08-09  
**Assessment type:** documented-capability audit; no account-level export experiment

The cross-section is not hand-picked by brand or expected outcome. It includes every currently designated EU Digital Markets Act core platform service in the following relational categories:

- social networks: TikTok, Facebook, Instagram, and LinkedIn;
- number-independent interpersonal communications: WhatsApp and Messenger;
- video sharing: YouTube.

The external selection frame is the [European Commission's 2026 DMA review](https://digital-markets-act.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2026-04/DMA%20Review%20Report_COM_2026_178_1_EN.pdf). Mastodon remains an outside control and is not counted in the seven-service result.

For each service, the ordinary user-accessible exit or handover route will be tested from official documentation against five questions:

1. Does it preserve the user's source content or records?
2. Does it preserve stable identifiers for the other involved actors and shared objects?
3. Does it preserve contribution, response, role, and history links as reconstructable relationships rather than isolated text?
4. Can another system import or otherwise reconstruct those relations without continuing dependence on the original service?
5. Are omissions or limitations explicit enough to distinguish absence from undocumented capability?

Outcomes are MITIGATED, EXPOSED, or UNDETERMINED. EXPOSED means the qualifying architecture pattern is documented in the standard path. It does not prove that a particular user has suffered an actual loss event.

## Predeclared 2B Platform Cross-Section — Result

**Freeze date:** 2026-08-09  
**Audit type:** documented-capability audit; no account-level export experiment  
**Population:** all seven currently DMA-designated relational core platform services in the predeclared categories: TikTok, Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Messenger, and YouTube.

### Decision rule

- **MITIGATED:** the documented standard handover preserves the source, stable actor or shared-object identifiers, the relevant contribution and history links, and a usable route to reconstruct those relations independently of the old service.
- **EXPOSED:** the documented standard handover preserves source content but explicitly limits or omits at least one of those relation requirements.
- **UNDETERMINED:** the public documentation does not describe the exported structure precisely enough to decide.
- Exposure is not evidence that an actual loss event occurred. It means the platform documents a handover boundary under which the approved 2B loss can occur.

### Results

| Service | Result | Documented finding |
|---|---|---|
| TikTok | EXPOSED | The portability schema exports posts, messages, followers, following, and comments, but several relation-bearing records use display names, text, dates, or service links rather than a complete set of stable actor, object, contribution, and reply identifiers. No documented independent reconstruction route completes that graph. |
| Facebook | EXPOSED | A direct export inspection demonstrated that content could remain while participant identity, full conversations, and stable links needed to match related objects were absent. Current direct-transfer documentation continues to describe transfers by content type, such as posts, notes, photos, and videos, rather than transfer of the complete social history. |
| Instagram | EXPOSED | Meta's documented direct-transfer route covers photos and videos. That route does not claim to transfer the actors, contributions, responses, and history connected to those objects. The broader account archive was not inspected in this pass. |
| LinkedIn | EXPOSED | The archive documents the member's own comments, reactions, messages, and first-degree connections. LinkedIn explicitly states that it provides the member's own personal data, not other members' data. URLs and profile URLs retain partial references, but no independent reconstruction or import of the shared history is documented. |
| YouTube | EXPOSED | Google's schema is comparatively rich and contains stable video, channel, comment, parent-comment, post, and playlist identifiers. However, the comments export is defined as comments the requesting user created, not the complete contribution history around the user's exported videos or posts. |
| Messenger | UNDETERMINED | Meta documents download of sent and received encrypted messages and attachments when secure storage is enabled. The public description does not specify enough about stable participant and conversation identifiers or independent import to decide whether the relation history is reconstructable outside Messenger. |
| WhatsApp | UNDETERMINED | Meta documents transfer of chat history between mobile operating systems and live interoperability with eligible third-party services. These preserve use within WhatsApp or enable future communication; the documentation inspected does not establish an independent transfer of complete historical relations to a replacement service. |

**Count:** 5 EXPOSED, 0 MITIGATED, 2 UNDETERMINED.
### Pattern finding

The platform pass supports the following bounded architectural pattern:

> An account-scoped or content-type-scoped handover can preserve digital source content while failing to transfer the stable actor, shared-object, contribution, response, and history links needed to reconstruct the same digital whole independently of the original service.

The direct Facebook export inspection makes this pattern **CAUSALLY_DEMONSTRATED for that tested implementation**. Current official documentation across five of the seven predeclared services makes **recurring exposure to the same pattern STRONGLY_SUPPORTED within this bounded platform set**. It does not prove that every export instance loses relations, that every function of a platform must be portable, or that the pattern occurs across the internet as a whole.

The causal mechanism is the handover boundary itself: when the exported unit is one account or one content type instead of a shared event graph, relations owned, recorded, or disclosed through other accounts can fall outside the exported whole.

### Red-team result

Four objections change the interpretation but do not remove the factual 2B possibility:

1. **Other people's privacy is a real constraint.** A safe replacement architecture cannot solve 2B by indiscriminately copying everybody's personal data.
2. **A service URL is not independent preservation.** It can help while the old service and identifier resolver remain available, but it does not by itself reconstruct the history after disappearance.
3. **Live interoperability is not historical portability.** It can prevent some future isolation without transferring the already accumulated history.
4. **Documentation is not an output test.** This pass establishes documented exposure. Only reproducible account-level export tests can verify the exact current files and fields.

### Candidate-2 conclusion after this pass

- **2A remains open:** no qualifying causal pattern has yet been established under the approved strict definition of total source loss.
- **2B advances:** one precise pattern is directly demonstrated in one implementation and documented as a recurring exposure across five of seven predeclared services.
- The platform cross-section supports breadth of exposure only. It does not supply a frequency estimate for actual 2B loss events.
