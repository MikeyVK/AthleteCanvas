**Version:** 0.2  
**Last updated:** 2026-08-08  
**Purpose:** Distinguish externally grounded evidence-synthesis practice from Ypsia-specific Protocol 0.1 experiments, subject to the Approved foundational research definitions.  
**Authority:** Informative until DEC-010 is Approved; it does not itself authorise pilot execution.

## 1. Why this document exists

Methodological vocabulary can create borrowed authority. Calling an activity a scoping review, independent review, saturation test, or evidence assessment does not make it rigorous merely because the term is established.

This document therefore separates:

1. recognised conduct or reporting guidance;
2. adaptations required by a cross-domain sociotechnical research object;
3. unvalidated Ypsia pilot rules that must be tested rather than presented as settled scientific method.

No source below validates the six-layer research object, equality framework, materiality threshold, rootness rubric, evidence-status taxonomy, or constitutional determination model.

### DEC-012 compatibility boundary

[FOUNDATIONAL_DEFINITIONS.md](FOUNDATIONAL_DEFINITIONS.md) 0.1 now governs the research object, problem structure, human-norm separation, architectural contribution, and admission boundary.

The former proposed six-layer sociotechnical Internet scope is no longer the primary causal research object. Its layers may still provide context or source-discovery coverage, but they cannot admit a candidate merely because a societal phenomenon interacts with a digital layer. Protocol, Pilot Manifest, register, materiality, and rootness rules must be checked against DEC-012 before approval or execution.

The existing source and counterevidence dossiers remain reusable. Their candidate formulations and statuses are not grandfathered into the revised research.

## 2. External methodological basis

| Basis | What it contributes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| [JBI Manual for Evidence Synthesis — Scoping Reviews](https://jbi-global.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/MANUAL/pages/355862497/10.%2BScoping%2Breviews) | An a-priori protocol; explicit questions and inclusion criteria; search strategy; source selection; data extraction; analysis; presentation; and a PCC-oriented scoping-review framework. | A universal minimum number of databases, a Ypsia evidence taxonomy, or constitutional relevance. |
| [PRISMA-ScR](https://www.prisma-statement.org/scoping) | A reporting checklist for scoping-review rationale, protocol, eligibility, information sources, search, selection, charting, appraisal, synthesis, limitations, and funding. | A conduct manual, quality certificate, risk-of-bias tool, or guarantee that the search is complete. |
| [PRISMA-S](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8270366/) | Transparent reporting of literature-search information sources, exact strategies, limits, dates, peer review, deduplication, and supplementary searching. | Proof that a reported search is sensitive, unbiased, or sufficient for every discipline. |
| [SWiM reporting guideline](https://www.bmj.com/content/368/bmj.l6890) | Transparent reporting when quantitative findings are synthesised without meta-analysis, including grouping, synthesis method, data presentation, and limitations. | Permission to pool incomparable claims or to replace design-appropriate appraisal with narrative judgement. |
| [Joint evidence-synthesis position on responsible AI use](https://jbi.global/news/article/global-evidence-leaders-mandate-responsible-ai-use) and [Cochrane's responsible-AI account](https://www.cochrane.org/about-us/news/how-cochrane-advancing-responsible-ai-evidence-synthesis) | Accountability, disclosure, justification, methodological soundness, transparency about limitations and bias, and human oversight when AI is used. | A finding that an LLM is equivalent to a human reviewer, that fresh context removes correlated model error, or that AI-only review is scientifically independent. |

These sources are methodological guidance and reporting standards, not empirical evidence about the Internet. Their applicability is reviewed again before any production protocol freeze because the online guidance can change.

## 3. Protocol components grounded in that basis

Protocol 0.1 adopts or operationalises the following established ideas:

- an explicit, versioned protocol before review execution;
- bounded questions, population or affected interests, concept, and context;
- predeclared eligibility criteria;
- reproducible search strategies and information-source reporting;
- explicit screening stages, exclusions, and flow counts;
- structured data charting and source-level traceability;
- separation of scoping or mapping from claim-level synthesis;
- transparent reporting of heterogeneity, limitations, null findings, and synthesis method;
- disclosure and justification of AI use;
- reviewer accountability and visible deviations.

Compliance with one reporting checklist never substitutes for executing the underlying method.

## 4. Ypsia-specific pilot hypotheses

The following are project-specific rules, not scientific consensus or requirements derived from the sources above:

- the proposed six-layer sociotechnical Internet scope;
- a default minimum of two relevant scholarly databases or indexes plus one official, legal, statistical, institutional, or technical repository;
- a fixed independent check of all full-text exclusions, high-topology records, and at least 20 percent of title and abstract exclusions;
- the separate empirical-evidence, formulation, normative-recognition, and counterevidence axes;
- the eight-dimension materiality rubric and its one-HIGH-or-two-MODERATE eligibility threshold;
- the seven-part rootness rubric and countermodel;
- the equality impact screen and DEC-004 full safeguard;
- three consecutive predeclared null-yield iterations as one necessary saturation condition;
- the claim-candidate-topology data model;
- role-separated fresh-context AI reviewers in the bounded pilot.

The pilot may show that any of these rules is too weak, too strong, too costly, ambiguous, or invalid. Such a result is a successful method finding, not a reason to conceal the failure.

## 5. AI-assisted review boundary

A fresh AI context can reduce conversational anchoring and author-role contamination. It cannot establish independence from:

- the same base model and training distribution;
- shared system instructions and tool limits;
- common retrieval blind spots;
- correlated hallucination or reasoning patterns;
- the primary researcher's framing and supplied corpus;
- absence of lived, disciplinary, legal, or local expertise.

Protocol 0.1 therefore uses **procedural independence** as a narrow term: the reviewer did not author the object and reviews it from a separately instantiated context. Every formal review and independent screening event records reviewer type, system, context locator, independence basis, shared-model or shared-team risk, and human-oversight status.

An AI same-model fresh-context review may be procedurally independent but must not be described as:

- independent human duplicate review;
- independent expert peer review;
- model-diverse replication;
- external validation.

Human decision authority cannot upgrade evidence. Human oversight provides accountability, not automatic subject-matter expertise. Before production work may claim human dual review or expert validation, the required human roles, expertise, conflicts, workload, and adjudication route must be explicitly approved and staffed.

## 6. Cross-domain adaptation rule

Issue 33 crosses technical systems, law, economics, social science, environmental evidence, ethics, and governance. One appraisal instrument cannot validly score all source types.

The scoping layer may use one shared discovery and traceability spine. Targeted syntheses must select design-appropriate appraisal and synthesis methods for the exact claim. Examples include empirical risk-of-bias tools, legal authority and jurisdiction analysis, technical threat-model or proof review, and explicit normative argument assessment.

Tool selection is frozen per workstream before appraisal. A source cannot receive a high-quality label merely because it is peer reviewed, official, recent, or frequently cited.

## 7. Claims permitted before the pilot

The current package may responsibly claim only that:

- it proposes a protocol influenced by recognised evidence-synthesis conduct and reporting guidance;
- it records its project-specific deviations and experiments;
- it has an auditable empty record structure;
- it still requires human scope and pilot authority;
- its AI review design has a declared correlated-independence limitation.

It may not yet claim that the Internet review is systematic, saturated, independently peer reviewed, scientifically validated, or complete.

## 8. Pilot evaluation obligations

The pilot report must separately evaluate:

- adherence to the externally grounded components;
- feasibility and reviewer agreement for each Ypsia-specific rule;
- false reassurance caused by categorical statuses;
- shared-model reviewer correlation and disagreements missed by AI review;
- tasks that require a human information specialist, domain expert, legal expert, or affected-interest perspective;
- whether “systematic scoping review” remains an honest label for each workstream;
- which deviations must be corrected before Protocol 1.0 can even be proposed.

No production-method approval follows automatically from completing the pilot.
